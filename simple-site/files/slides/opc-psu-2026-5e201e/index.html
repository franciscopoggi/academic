Off-Protocol Communication
Francisco Poggi
University of Mannheim
Pennsylvania State University · 2026

Motivation

  • Communication is sometimes organized around a protocol: who speaks, when, what messages are allowed.
    • E.g., peer review, courtroom procedure, expert committees, internal reporting.
  • Protocols shape information transmission and hence outcomes.
  • However, participants may also communicate off protocol:
    • referee and editor at a conference dinner;
    • ex parte communication with the judge;
    • employee and supervisor in the hallway.

Questions. When does off-protocol communication undermine the protocol?
How can protocols be designed to be robust to off-protocol communication?

Challenge. The design of the protocol determines what can be communicated on protocol, but it also changes what can be credibly communicated off protocol.

Motivating Example: Expert Advice with Career Concerns

  • A manager must choose an action $a\in\{L,R\}$.
  • The correct action depends on an equally likely state $\omega\in\{L,R\}$.
  • There are two employees: one is the expert, the other is uninformed. Each privately knows his role; the manager regards both assignments as equally likely.
  • The expert observes a signal that matches the state with probability $p_E$.
  • The manager also promotes one of the two employees, $\pi\in\{1,2\}$.
Payoffs
Everyone receives $1$ when $a=\omega$.
An employee receives an additional bonus $B>0$ if promoted.
The manager receives an additional $D>0$ if she promotes the expert.

Throughout the example: $\;p_E=0.8,\qquad B=0.4.$

An Anonymity Protocol

Protocol
Each employee reports his private information to a black box, which recommends an action:
  • one employee claims to be the expert: the action matching his reported signal;
  • both claim the same role: an action drawn uniformly at random.
The manager sees the recommendation, not the reports, and promotes an employee at random.

Truthful reporting pays $\;U_E=U_Q=p_E+\tfrac B2=1$.

Nobody gains by misreporting (given obedience):

  • an expert who reverses his signal cuts accuracy to $0.2$;
  • an expert who claims to be uninformed, to $0.5$;
  • an uninformed employee who claims to be the expert, to $0.5$;
  • no deviation changes the deviator's promotion probability.

Obedience (given truthful reporting): the recommendation is correct with probability $0.8$, and nothing identifies the expert, so the manager is indifferent whom to promote.

⇒ The protocol has a truthful–obedient equilibrium.

Off-Protocol Speech

An expert with signal $L$ approaches the manager privately:

"I chose my report at random. I am the expert, and my signal was $L$. Choose $L$ and promote me, whatever the protocol recommended."

If the claim is true, the manager wants to comply.
But the credibility of the speech depends on who benefits from it.

Design Changes Credibility

Change the protocol: the black box now also recommends a promotion.

  • one employee claims to be the expert:
    • the action matching his reported signal;
    • promote him with probability $y\in(\tfrac12,1]$;
  • both claim the same role: action and promotion drawn uniformly at random.

There is still a truthful–obedient equilibrium; accuracy stays at $p_E$.
Consider the same speech.

For $y>\tfrac34$, the uninformed employee also benefits: the speech loses its self-selection rationale.

Overview
This Paper

What We Do

  • Study communication protocols when senders can also talk to the receiver off protocol.
    • A designer chooses the protocol: who communicates, what messages are allowed, how they are processed.
    • She cannot prevent a sender from addressing the receiver directly (privately, cheaply).
    • No commitment: the receiver cannot promise to ignore what she hears outside the protocol.
  • Propose a robustness criterion based on the credibility of speeches.
    • A speech makes claims about the sender's type and behavior, and recommends what the receiver should do.
    • It is credible if
      • the receiver would comply when taking it at face value;
      • complying is profitable to the sender only if the speech is true.
Robustness criterion
No credible off-protocol speech.

Preview of Results

  • One sender.
    • The revelation principle survives: direct mechanisms are without loss, also for robustness.
    • Robustness coincides with Farrell's neologism-proofness.
  • Several senders.
    • The revelation principle fails: the same outcome can be robust under one protocol and fragile under another.
    • Two forces: the reports available to senders, and the information delivered to the receiver.
  • Experts with career concerns.
    • Characterize what direct mechanisms can implement when they must be robust to off-protocol communication.
    • Beyond direct mechanisms: some outcomes are robust in every protocol, some in none, and some only in a richer protocol.

Farrell (1993) in a Nutshell

Cheap talk: one sender, two equally likely types, three actions. Payoffs $(u_0,u_1)$ = (receiver, sender).

$U$$C$$D$
$t_1$$(3,3)$$(2,2)$$(0,0)$
$t_2$$(0,0)$$(2,2)$$(3,3)$

Receiver's best responses: $U$ against $t_1$, $D$ against $t_2$, the safe action $C$ when $\Pr(t_1)\in[\tfrac13,\tfrac23]$.

  • Babbling equilibrium. No information, the receiver plays $C$, both types get $2$.
  • Separating equilibrium. The receiver plays $U$ and $D$, both types get $3$.

"I am $t_1$."  Taken at face value, the receiver plays $U$. Who would say this?

Neologism-proofness (Farrell 1993)
Fix an equilibrium with interim payoffs $U^*(\theta)$. A neologism "my type is in $S$" is answered by the receiver's best response $a(S)$ given $S$. It is credible if exactly the types in $S$ strictly gain, $\;u_1(a(S),\theta)>U^*(\theta)\iff\theta\in S$. An equilibrium is neologism-proof if it admits no credible neologism.
Two minutes. The point to land is the self-selection logic: a statement is believed if, and only if, believing it would help exactly the types making it. Babbling is not neologism-proof, since $(\{t_1\},U)$ is credible; the separating equilibrium is, since nobody can gain more than $3$. Two conventions worth stating aloud, since they recur: the receiver takes the claim at face value (prior conditional on $S$), and self-selection is strict, so an indifferent outsider does not block the speech. The payoff matrix returns with a conflict of interest on the existence slide, where no equilibrium survives the test.
Part II
General Model

Setup

  • A receiver, $i=0$, and $n$ senders $i=1,\dots,n$.
  • Sender $i$ has a type $\theta_i\in\Theta_i$; common prior $\mu\in\Delta(\Theta)$. The receiver has no private information.
  • The receiver chooses $a\in A$. All sets are finite.
Payoffs and timing
Payoffs: $u_i(a,\theta)$ for $i=0,\dots,n$.
Timing: types realize $\to$ communication $\to$ receiver acts.

Example: $\Theta_i=\{L,R,Q\}$, $A=\{L,R\}\times\{1,2\}$.

Protocols

Protocol
A protocol $P$ is a tuple $\big(\{M_i\}_{i=1}^{n},\,M_0,\,\tau\big)$.
  1. Senders simultaneously report $m_i\in M_i$ to a black box.
  2. The receiver sees $m_0$, drawn according to $\tau:\prod_i M_i\to\Delta(M_0)$.
  3. The receiver acts.
Includes
Direct mechanisms: $M_i=\Theta_i$, $M_0=A$.
Transparent protocols: $M_0=\prod_i M_i$, $\tau$ the identity.

Equilibrium and the Revelation Principle

Strategies:

$$\sigma_i:\Theta_i\to\Delta(M_i), \qquad \sigma_0:M_0\to\Delta(A).$$

An equilibrium is a BNE. It induces an outcome: a distribution on $\Theta\times A$.

Revelation principle (Myerson 1982, Forges 1986)
Absent off-protocol speech, every equilibrium outcome of every protocol is the truthful–obedient outcome of some incentive-compatible direct protocol.
  • Fix a protocol and an equilibrium with outcome $\eta\in\Delta(\Theta\times A)$ and conditionals $\hat{\tau}:\Theta\to\Delta(A)$. The direct mechanism $\hat{\tau}$ has a truthful–obedient equilibrium.
  • So if we only care about implementable outcomes, it is without loss to focus on truthful and obedient direct mechanisms.

Off-Protocol Communication

  • Fix a protocol $P$ and an equilibrium $(\sigma,\sigma_0)$; let $U_i^*(\theta_i)$ be sender $i$'s interim payoff.
  • After the protocol delivers $m_0$, but before the action is chosen, a sender may address the receiver privately.
Confession by sender $i$: $(S,\hat{\sigma},\hat{\sigma}_0)$
$\emptyset\neq S\subseteq\Theta_i$: the claimed types.
$\hat{\sigma}:S\to\Delta(M_i)$: the claimed reporting strategy.
$\hat{\sigma}_0:M_0\to\Delta(A)$: the proposed adjustment.
"I am in $S$, I reported according to $\hat{\sigma}$, now do $\hat{\sigma}_0$."
The speeches we have seen

Credible Speeches

Let $V_i(\theta_i;\hat{\sigma}_0):=\max_{m_i\in M_i}U_i\big((\theta_i,m_i),\hat{\sigma}_0\big)$: the best type $\theta_i$ can do by choosing its report with the adjustment in place.

A confession $(S,\hat{\sigma},\hat{\sigma}_0)$ is credible if
  1. Honest play. $\operatorname{supp}\hat{\sigma}(\theta_i)\subseteq\arg\max_{m_i}U_i\big((\theta_i,m_i),\hat{\sigma}_0\big)$ for every $\theta_i\in S$.
  2. Self-selection. $V_i(\theta_i;\hat{\sigma}_0)>U_i^*(\theta_i)\iff\theta_i\in S$. Types are tested at their best report, not at equilibrium play.
  3. Receiver optimality. At every consistent $m_0$, $\hat{\sigma}_0(m_0)$ is optimal for the receiver taking the claim $(S,\hat{\sigma})$ at face value.
  4. Reversion. At every inconsistent $m_0$ the speech is ignored: $\hat{\sigma}_0(m_0)=\sigma_0(m_0)$.
Robustness
An equilibrium is confession-proof if it admits no credible confession.

Part III
Results

Revelation Principle with One Sender

Let $n=1$. Fix a protocol and an equilibrium. The canonical DRP has a truthful–obedient equilibrium that induces the same outcome. It is confession-proof iff the original equilibrium is.

Proof intuition.

  • With $n=1$, the claim about what was played in the mechanism has no bite: reports map one-to-one to action recommendations.
  • Everything boils down to a claim $(S,\lambda)$: a set of types and a lottery over actions.
  • Credibility only depends on the comparison between $\lambda$ and $U^*$.
This strengthens TM-03's original theorem. All type, message, and action sets are finite, and the credibility definition is belief-free. Neither unique per-type receiver best replies nor pure or onto equilibrium sender strategies are required.

Let $B_S=\bigcap_{\theta\in S}\arg\max_a u_0(a,\theta)$. Given a credible confession $(S,\hat\sigma,\hat\sigma_0)$, choose a claimant $\bar\theta$ and a message $\bar m$ in its claimed support. The induced lottery is $\lambda(a)=\sum_z\tau(z\mid\bar m)\hat\sigma_0(a\mid z)$. All its deliveries are consistent, so belief-free optimality puts the support of $\lambda$ inside $B_S$.

Honest play and strict self-selection imply $u_1(\lambda,\bar\theta)>U^*(\bar\theta)$. Every original outsider could send the same message and receive the same lottery, so none gains. Thus $\varnothing\ne G(\lambda)\subseteq S$, and $\operatorname{supp}\lambda\subseteq B_S\subseteq B_{G(\lambda)}$. Shrinking the type set is the step that removes the need for uniqueness.

Conversely, if a lottery is receiver-optimal for every type in its nonempty set of strict gainers, ask each claimant to use the same full-support distribution on the protocol's sender messages. Every reachable delivery becomes consistent. Propose the same lottery at each such delivery and revert at unreachable ones. Every sender message now induces the same lottery, so honesty is automatic and self-selection is exact.

The characterization depends only on preferences and $U^*$. The canonical direct mechanism preserves the outcome and hence $U^*$. Truthfulness follows by replicating original mixed deviations; obedience follows because a recommended action is optimal at every original posterior pooled into that recommendation.

Under unique receiver favorites, the common best-response set of any feasible claim is a singleton, so the lottery is a pure action and the original pure-neologism equivalence follows. With ties, pure neologisms may fail to detect profitable lottery confessions.

Do not infer that the claimed strategy can be held at equilibrium play. Full-support scrambling may be necessary. The simple-speech equivalence on the following slide needs separate correction; it is not used by this proof.

One Sender: Confessions and Neologisms

Neologism (Farrell 1993)
A neologism is a pair $(S,\lambda)$ with $S\subseteq\Theta$ and $\lambda\in\Delta(A)$. It is credible if exactly the types in $S$ prefer $\lambda$ to equilibrium, $\;u_1(\lambda,\theta)>U^*(\theta)\iff\theta\in S$, and $\lambda$ is receiver-optimal for every type in $S$.
Set $n=1$. Fix a protocol and an equilibrium. $$\text{confession-proof}\;\iff\;\text{neologism-proof}.$$
  • Confession $\Rightarrow$ neologism. A claimant's report induces a lottery $\lambda$ over actions. Claimants gain; outsiders can imitate, so none can prefer $\lambda$.
  • Neologism $\Rightarrow$ confession. A full-support claim implements $\lambda$ at every reachable delivery.

Every credible confession has a credible type-only counterpart.

Existence
Theorem 2 of the talk; TM-04. Both directions are statements about existence of credible speeches, so the proof directions are "confession implies neologism" and "neologism implies confession"; their contrapositives give the two halves of the equivalence.

Confession implies neologism. Take a credible confession $(S,\hat\sigma,\hat\sigma_0)$. With one sender, belief-free optimality has no belief content: at every consistent delivery the adjustment must lie in the common best-response set of $S$, which under uniqueness is a single action $c$. A claimant's claimed message reaches only consistent deliveries, so it yields $c$; honest play and strict self-selection give $u_1(c,\theta)>U^*(\theta)$ on $S$. An outsider can send that same message and also get $c$, so self-selection bounds $u_1(c,\theta')\le U^*(\theta')$ off $S$. Hence $(S,c)$ is a credible neologism.

Neologism implies confession. Let every type in $S$ claim the same full-support distribution over the protocol's messages, so every reachable delivery is consistent; adjust to $c$ there and revert elsewhere. Every message by every type then yields $c$, so honest play is automatic and self-selection is exactly the neologism's.

Implementation qualification. The theorem characterizes when a credible confession exists; it does not say the confession can hold the claimed strategy at equilibrium play. Full-support scrambling of the claimed strategy may be necessary. In particular, "confession-proof iff simple-speech-proof" is false: TM-04 §6 has an equilibrium that admits no credible simple speech (claimed strategy equal to equilibrium play) yet admits a credible confession. The old inference "a credible simple speech yields a credible neologism, hence simple-speech-proof implies neologism-proof" had its contrapositive reversed. A sufficient condition for simple speeches to suffice is coverage: each claimant's equilibrium messages reach every reachable delivery (TM-04 §7).

Extension without uniqueness. Drop unique best responses and replace the action $c$ by a lottery $\lambda$ over actions: a credible lottery neologism is $(G(\lambda),\lambda)$ with $G(\lambda)=\{\theta:u_1(\lambda,\theta)>U^*(\theta)\}$ nonempty and $\lambda$ supported on actions optimal against every type in $G(\lambda)$. Then, with one sender and belief-free credibility, confession-proof iff no credible lottery neologism (TM-03 §8, TM-04 §3). Extract $\lambda$ from a claimant's message, shrink the claim to its exact gainers, transfer by full-support scrambling. With receiver ties, pure neologisms can miss a profitable lottery confession (TM-04 §8).

With several senders the equivalence fails: receiver optimality conditions on the others' types through the claimed report and the delivery, which is exactly what the expert's sabotage-and-confess speech exploits in the application.

Receiver Information Can Make Speech Non-Credible

Two senders with independent, uniform types $\theta_1\in\{a,b\}$, $\theta_2\in\{L,R\}$. Sender 2 is indifferent and reports truthfully. The receiver chooses $e$, $h$ or $k$.

Three reveals: first the claim and its response with no information, then the row with disclosure, then the credibility comparison and intuition.

Both protocols are sender-direct. Both senders report truthfully, so sender strategies are pure and onto. Sender 1's report never affects deliveries, making every claimed report, including every mixed strategy, optimal. Sender 2 always gets zero. The example isolates receiver information from the previous slide's sender-message effect.

Equilibrium obedience is strict. Without information, receiver payoffs from $e,h,k$ are $2,5/4,3/4$. After observing $L$, they are $2,3/2,0$. After observing $R$, they are $2,1,3/2$. Thus the receiver chooses $e$ in either protocol, and every sender type gets zero.

With coarse information, the confession is $S=\{a\}$ with adjustment $h$ at the constant delivery and any claimed sender 1 report. Conditional on $a$, receiver payoffs from $e,h,k$ are $2,5/2,3/2$, so $h$ is uniquely optimal. Type $a$ gets $1$ and type $b$ gets $-1$, making self-selection exact. This establishes a credible confession.

With disclosure, a claim of $a$ forces $h$ at $L$ and $k$ at $R$, each uniquely optimal. Type $a$ gets $1$ and outsider $b$ gets $(-1+3)/2=1$. Asking for $h$ at both deliveries would deter $b$, but the receiver rejects that instruction at $R$, where $k$ gives $3$ instead of $2$.

The informative equilibrium is confession-proof, not just immune to this particular speech. For any claim $S=\{a\}$, the same forced responses attract $b$. For $S=\{b\}$, receiver optimality forces $e$ at both deliveries, so nobody gains. For $S=\{a,b\}$, receiver optimality requires $h$ conditional on $a$ and $e$ conditional on $b$ at $L$. These strict requirements are incompatible. Sender 2 never strictly gains. This exhausts all type sets, mixed claims, and mixed adjustments under belief-free credibility.

All receiver deliveries occur on path. Both informative deliveries are consistent with every confession by sender 1, so reversion plays no role in the argument. Sender 1's payoff depends only on its own type and the action.

The canonical truthful-obedient DRP always recommends $e$ and therefore conceals sender 2's type. It admits the coarse protocol's confession. Because every type profile has positive probability, any truthful-obedient DRP implementing the constant-$e$ outcome must recommend $e$ at every report profile. Hence no truthful-obedient DRP implements this outcome confession-proofly.

The result shows that disclosure can improve robustness, not that more disclosure always does. Suggested verbal link: richer sender messages change an outsider's available deviations under a fixed adjustment; richer receiver information changes which adjustment the receiver can rationally follow.
Application
Expert Advice with Career Concerns

Expert Advice with Career Concerns: Model

  • Players: two senders $i=1,2$ and one receiver.
  • One sender is an expert ($E$), the other a quack ($Q$). Let $e\in\{1,2\}$ be the expert's identity.
  • Roles are private; each assignment is equally likely.
  • Payoff-relevant state $\omega\in\{L,R\}$, uniform prior.
  • Each sender observes a conditionally independent binary signal $s_i\in\{l,r\}$ with precision $p_E$ and $p_Q$.
    • For exposition, $p_Q=1/2$: the quack is completely uninformed.
  • The receiver chooses an action $a\in\{L,R\}$ and a promotion $\pi\in\{1,2\}$.
Payoffs
Everyone receives $1$ if $a=\omega$.
Sender $i$ gets an additional bonus $B>0$ iff promoted ($\pi=i$).
The receiver gets an additional bonus $D>0$ if the expert is promoted ($\pi=e$).

Direct Revelation Mechanisms

  • A direct revelation mechanism asks each sender to report whether they are the expert, and their signal realization.
    • Given the uninformativeness of the quack's signal, we can simplify to $\Theta_i=\{L,R,Q\}$.
  • It recommends an action and a promotion to the receiver, $(\hat{a},\hat{\pi})\in\{L,R\}\times\{1,2\}$.
  • A direct revelation mechanism is thus a map from reports to a lottery over recommendations, $$\tau:\ \Theta_1\times\Theta_2 \;\to\; \Delta\big(\{L,R\}\times\{1,2\}\big).$$

The Revelation Principle (Myerson 1982) justifies the focus on direct revelation mechanisms (absent speeches).

A Symmetric Family

  • We focus on a symmetric family of direct revelation mechanisms:
    • both senders are treated symmetrically;
    • both states and actions are treated symmetrically.
  • Under truthful reports, a symmetric mechanism is summarized by two numbers:† $$x=\Pr(\hat{a}=\omega \mid \text{truthful reports}),\qquad y=\Pr(\hat{\pi}=e \mid \text{truthful reports}).$$
  • We must also define what happens after inconsistent reports.*
Inconsistent reports
Both senders claim to be the quack: $(\hat{a},\hat{\pi})$ drawn uniformly at random.
Both senders claim to be the expert:
  • reports do not coincide: $(\hat{a},\hat{\pi})$ drawn uniformly at random;
  • reports coincide: the recommended action $\hat{a}$ matches the common report with probability $\alpha$.
($\alpha=1/2$: every inconsistency leads to a uniform draw.)
†The correlation between $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{\pi}$ is not payoff relevant: payoffs depend only on the marginals, which is why $x$ and $y$ suffice.
*A similar application can be constructed with independent types. There are then no inconsistent reports, but more cases to consider.
Symmetry is without loss: the environment is invariant under permutations of sender identities and simultaneous exchanges of state, signal, and action labels.

Truthfulness and Obedience ($\alpha=1/2$)(general $\alpha$)

Obedience
The receiver follows the recommendation iff $x\ge \tfrac12$ and $y\ge \tfrac12$.
Expert truthfulness
$$\underbrace{U_E \;:=\; x + y\,B}_{\text{truthful report}} \;\ge\; \max\Big\{\underbrace{\tfrac12+\tfrac{B}{2}}_{\text{claim to be the quack}},\;\; \underbrace{(1-x)+y\,B}_{\text{flip the signal}}\Big\}$$ Implied by obedience. Claiming $Q$ makes the reports inconsistent (uniform draw).
Quack truthfulness
$$\underbrace{U_Q \;:=\; x + (1-y)\,B}_{\text{truthful report}} \;\ge\; \underbrace{\tfrac12+\tfrac{B}{2}}_{\text{claim to be the expert}}$$
$$\underbrace{U_Q \;:=\; x + (1-y)\,B}_{\text{truthful report}} \;\ge\; \underbrace{\colorbox{#f3d9da}{$\textcolor{#b5121b}{\tilde{x}(\alpha)}$}+\tfrac{B}{2}}_{\text{claim to be the expert}}$$
Not implied by obedience. Claiming to be the expert also makes the reports inconsistent (uniform draw). $\tilde{x}(\alpha)$: accuracy of the recommended action when the quack pretends to be the expert, $$\tilde{x}(\alpha)=\tfrac14+\tfrac12\big[\alpha\,p_E+(1-\alpha)(1-p_E)\big],\qquad \alpha\in[0,1].$$
Last step switches the slide from $\alpha=1/2$ to general $\alpha$. Obedience and expert truthfulness are untouched: the expert's deviation to $Q$ yields two $Q$ claims, which is a uniform draw for every $\alpha$. Only the quack's deviation (both claim expert) is affected: $\tilde{x}(\alpha)=\tfrac12[\alpha p_E+(1-\alpha)(1-p_E)]+\tfrac14$, so $\tilde{x}(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12$ and $\tilde{x}\in[\tfrac34-\tfrac{p_E}{2},\,\tfrac14+\tfrac{p_E}{2}]$.
$\tilde{x}(\alpha)$: derivation

Symmetric Mechanisms in the Unit Square ($\alpha=1/2$)

  • By obedience, $x,y\ge\tfrac12$: an obedient, symmetric mechanism is a point $$(2x-1,\;2y-1)\in[0,1]^2.$$
    • horizontal: decision accuracy;
    • vertical: how often the expert is promoted.
  • Feasibility. The decision cannot be more accurate than the expert's signal: $x\le p_E$.
  • Quack's iso-payoff lines. $$U_Q = x+(1-y)\,B$$ Slope $1/B$; $U_Q$ rises to the south-east.
  • Quack truthfulness. $U_Q\ge\tfrac12+\tfrac B2$, its value at the origin: the region below the line through the origin.
General $\alpha$

Designer Objectives

So far we have only looked at what can be implemented. Let us give the designer an objective.

  • The designer gets $1$ if $a=\omega$, plus $b$ if the expert is promoted.*
  • Expected payoff under truthful reports: $\;x + b\,y$.
  • $b>0$: wants the expert promoted.   $b<0$: wants the expert not promoted.
Weak career concerns: $B < 2p_E-1$
The designer attains his maximum payoff: truthfulness does not bind.
  • Full credit if $b>0$: $\;y=1$.
  • No credit if $b<0$: $\;y=\tfrac12$.
Strong career concerns: $B > 2p_E-1$
Quack truthfulness binds when the designer wants to promote the expert.
  • Maximum credit if $b>0$: $\;y<1$.
  • No credit if $b<0$: $\;y=\tfrac12$.

In every case the decision is as accurate as it can be: $x=p_E$.

*The designer's and the receiver's objectives may differ: the receiver gets $D>0$ from promoting the expert, the designer gets $b$, of either sign.
Robustness
Off-Protocol Communication

Two Types of Speeches

"Simple" speeches: about private information only
"I'm the expert. Promote me."
"I'm the expert, my signal was $s$. Play $s$ and promote me."
"Complex" speeches, or confessions: about private information and behavior
"I'm the expert, and I lied in my report. Flip the recommended action; and since a quack would not benefit from asking you this, promote me."

Simple Speech

"I'm the expert, got signal $L$. Play $L$ and promote me."
The expert strictly gains iff
$$U_E \;:=\; x + y\,B \;<\; p_E + B$$ i.e. iff $(x,y)\neq(p_E,1)$: at full credit he has nothing to gain.
The quack strictly gains iff
$$U_Q \;<\; \tfrac12 + B$$ An indifferent quack does not block the speech.
Credible iff the expert strictly gains and the quack does not
Robustness requires $\;U_Q < \tfrac12 + B$, unless $(x,y)=(p_E,1)$.

Self-selection also requires that the expert with signal $R$ does not strictly gain, $U_E \ge (1-p_E)+B$.
This never binds: if he strictly gains, so does the quack.

Complex Speech: the Expert's Confession

"I'm the expert and was not truthful. Do not follow the action recommendation, and promote me."
The expert strictly gains iff
$$U_E \;<\; x + B$$ i.e. iff $y<1$.
The quack strictly gains iff
$$U_Q \;<\; \max\big\{\,1-x,\;1-\tilde{x}(\alpha)\big\} + B$$ He picks his report: claim the quack role ($1-x$) or the expert role ($1-\tilde{x}(\alpha)$).
Robustness requires
$$U_Q \;<\; \max\big\{\,1-x,\;1-\tilde{x}(\alpha)\big\} + B\qquad\text{unless } y=1.$$

The Quack's Confession and Binding Constraints

"I'm the quack and I claimed to be the expert. Thus, flip the action and don't promote me."
  • Strictly profitable iff $\;U_Q < 1-\tilde{x}(\alpha)$.
  • Not binding for $\tilde{x}(\alpha)=\tfrac12$: it reads $U_Q<\tfrac12$, while quack truthfulness already gives $U_Q\ge\tfrac12+\tfrac B2$.
Binding constraints for $\tilde{x}(\alpha)=\tfrac12$, away from full credit $(x,y)\neq(p_E,1)$
Incentive compatibility and robustness amount to $$U_Q \;\in\; \Big[\tfrac12+\tfrac{B}{2}\,,\;\tfrac12+B\Big).$$

Full credit $(x,y)=(p_E,1)$: the expert cannot strictly gain from either speech. Treated separately.

Robustness to the Simple Speech ($\alpha=1/2$)

  • Quack truthfulness: $U_Q\ \ge\ \tfrac12+\tfrac B2$.
  • Robustness: $U_Q\ <\ \tfrac12+B$ (boundary excluded: dashed).
  • Mechanisms with accurate decisions and little credit for the expert invite "I'm the expert, promote me."

Intuition

$\tilde{x}(\alpha)$ plays a dual role:

  • punish on-protocol deviations by the quack;
  • make off-protocol deviations by the quack more attractive, rendering the expert's deviation non-credible.

Both effects push $\tilde{x}(\alpha)$ down.

Binding constraints
Obedience, and two constraints on the quack's payoff.
Two ways to lower the quack's payoff $\;U_Q = x + (1-y)\,B$
Promote him less often (higher $y$): give the quack less credit.
Decide less accurately (lower $x$): reduce the precision of the action.

Designer Payoffs under Robustness

The designer maximizes $\;x + b\,y\;$ subject to robustness.

Intermediate career concerns: $\;p_E-\tfrac12 < B < 2p_E-1$
  • $b>0$: full credit, $\;(x,y)=(p_E,\,1)$.
  • $b\in(-B,0)$: minimum credit, $\;(x,y)\to(p_E,\,\underline{y})$, $\;\underline{y}=\tfrac{p_E-1/2}{B}$.
  • $b<-B$: no credit and garble, $\;(x,y)\to(\underline{p},\,\tfrac12)$, $\;\underline{p}=\tfrac{1+B}{2}$.

$\underline{y}$ and $\underline{p}$ solve $U_Q=\tfrac12+B$ on the edges $x=p_E$ and $y=\tfrac12$. The frontier itself is excluded: these payoffs are suprema, approached from inside.

Protocol-Independent Results

Now allow arbitrary finite protocols, with inference at inconsistent deliveries.

Let $\rho_y$ be the outcome that follows the expert's signal and promotes the expert with probability $y$:

  • $\rho_{1/2}$: no credit;
  • $\rho_{1}$: full credit.
Proposition (full-credit robustness)
Every equilibrium implementation of $\rho_1$ is confession-proof.
Each type already gets the most a credible confession could give him: $p_E+B$ for the expert, $p_E$ for the quack.
Proposition (no-credit fragility)
If $0<B\le 2p_E-1$, every equilibrium implementation of $\rho_{1/2}$ admits a credible confession.
$E_s$ claims a full-support report; an imitating quack gets at most $\tfrac12(p_E+B)+\tfrac12 p_E=p_E+\tfrac B2=U^*$.

The Limit of Direct Revelation

Proposition (failure of direct reduction)
Suppose $\;\tfrac12 < y < \min\Big\{\dfrac{p_E-\frac12}{B},\,1\Big\}$. The outcome $\rho_y$ has a confession-proof implementation in a finite protocol, but no confession-proof truthful–obedient direct implementation.
  • Direct mechanisms. An expert can randomize his report between $E_L$ and $E_R$, making every recommendation pair consistent with his claim. "Choose $s$, promote me" pays $p_E+B$, $\;1-p_E+B$, $\;\tfrac12+B$ to $E_s$, $E_{-s}$, $Q$. Under the conditions, only $E_s$ strictly gains: credible.
  • A richer protocol adds reports that make imitation attractive. Against any expert confession a quack gets $p_E+B$ when the colleague's signal matches the claim, and $p_E$ otherwise: the contradiction exposes him, the colleague's signal is followed, no promotion. Imitation pays $p_E+\tfrac B2>p_E+(1-y)B=U_Q$: self-selection fails.

$U_Q>p_E$ rules out quacks as claimants, not as imitators: no reduction to direct mechanisms, nor to the benchmark family.

A Protocol That Works

Reports $\;M_i=\{E_L,\,E_R,\,Q,\,Q_L,\,Q_R\}$, where $Q_s$ reads "I am the quack, and my colleague's signal is $s$."

$E_t$ and $Q$decision $t$; promote $i$ with probability $y$, the colleague otherwise
$Q_s$ and $Q$as $E_s$ and $Q$

  • On path: truthful reporting, only the first row is reached, outcome $\rho_y$. Truthful reporting is a best response: $Q_s$ pays the quack at most $p_E<U_Q$; an expert claim pays him $\tfrac12+\tfrac B2\le U_Q$.
  • Confession-proof: against any expert claim $E_s$ by $i$, the quack reports $Q_s$. If the colleague's signal is $s$, the delivery is consistent with the claim and the adjustment pays him $p_E+B$; if it is $-s$, the delivery exposes him, the manager follows $-s$ and promotes the colleague: $p_E$.
  • Imitation pays $\;p_E+\tfrac B2>p_E+(1-y)B=U_Q$: self-selection fails for every expert confession.

Conclusion

  • Incentive compatibility is not enough when participants can talk outside the protocol. A sender can change her behavior inside the protocol and explain it outside. Robustness requires ruling out credible confessions.
  • Expert advice. Under weak career concerns, concealing expertise can require less accurate decisions. Strong career concerns can help concealment by making the expert's speech attractive to the quack.
  • One sender. The revelation principle survives, and every credible confession has a neologism counterpart.
  • Multiple senders: protocol details matter. Richer message spaces and more receiver information can undermine credibility by making speeches attractive to outsiders.

Truthfulness and Obedience (general $\alpha$)

The designer has an extra instrument: punish inconsistent reports. Obedience and expert truthfulness are untouched.

Quack truthfulness
$U_Q \;\ge\; \tilde{x}(\alpha) + \tfrac{B}{2}$, where $\tilde{x}(\alpha)$ is the accuracy of the recommended action when the expert tells the truth and the quack pretends to be the expert.
Derivation of $\tilde{x}(\alpha)$
Both claim the expert role; the expert reports truthfully. The quack's signal is noise, so the reports coincide with probability $\tfrac12$, independently of the state.
Coincide: the action follows the common report w.p. $\alpha$; that report is the expert's signal, correct w.p. $p_E$ — accuracy $\alpha p_E+(1-\alpha)(1-p_E)$.  Differ: uniform draw — accuracy $\tfrac12$.
$\tilde{x}(\alpha)\;=\;\tfrac12\big[\alpha\,p_E+(1-\alpha)(1-p_E)\big]+\tfrac14,\qquad\alpha\in[0,1].$

Hence $\tilde{x}(0)=\tfrac34-\tfrac{p_E}{2}$, $\;\tilde{x}(\tfrac12)=\tfrac12$, $\;\tilde{x}(1)=\tfrac14+\tfrac{p_E}{2}$:  $\tilde{x}(\alpha)\in\big[\tfrac34-\tfrac{p_E}{2},\;\tfrac14+\tfrac{p_E}{2}\big]$.

Does a Mediator Rescue Existence?

With a mediator the set of communication equilibria is much larger: e.g. $t_1\mapsto\tfrac12 U+\tfrac12 C$, $t_2\mapsto C$ is incentive compatible.

No. In every incentive-compatible direct mechanism:

  • $U^*(t_1)<3$: giving $t_1$ the action $U$ for sure makes $U$ obedient only if $t_2$ rarely gets it — and then $t_2$ claims to be $t_1$.
  • $U^*(t_2)\ge 1$: $t_2$ can always claim $t_1$, and obedience keeps $t_1$'s lottery valuable enough to him (an LP; the minimum is exactly $1$).
So "I'm $t_1$; play $U$" is a credible neologism against every IC direct mechanism
By Theorems 1–2, no equilibrium of any protocol is confession-proof.

Confession-proofness is a Farrell-type test and inherits Farrell's non-existence. The mediator does not help.

Same strict self-selection convention as the application: $t_2$ is indifferent to imitating "I am $t_1$; play $U$", and the speech is still credible. Under the alternative rule (indifferent outsiders block) the tight mechanism $t_1\mapsto\tfrac12U+\tfrac14C+\tfrac14D$, $t_2\mapsto\tfrac12C+\tfrac12D$ would defeat non-existence.

Interpretation through an Explicit Speech Channel

Expanded game $\Gamma(P,\hat{M})$. Each sender submits a report $m_i$ and a private word $\hat{m}_i\in\hat{M}_i$ (finite, with a null word); the receiver sees $(m_0,\hat{m})$.

Availability of speech alone has no bite
Every equilibrium has a silent extension (words ignored), and the expanded game is itself a protocol: adding speech changes nothing by itself. Discipline must come from how unused words are read.
Credible-speech convention
Each non-null word gets a literal meaning: a confession, or none. An unused word meaning a confession credible relative to $(\sigma,\sigma_0)$ is answered with $\hat{\sigma}_0(m_0)$ at consistent deliveries and $\sigma_0(m_0)$ at inconsistent ones; nothing else is restricted.
Proposition (speech-channel interpretation)
$(\sigma,\sigma_0)$ is confession-proof $\iff$ for every finite language $\hat{M}$ and every assignment of literal meanings, $(\sigma,\sigma_0)$ has a silent equilibrium extension in $\Gamma(P,\hat{M})$ that satisfies the credible-speech convention.
